<Aside type="note" title="Read this when">

You want to see what your agents do while they work, or know exactly why a call was stopped. New in 0.17.0.

</Aside>

Both read the audit log only, in your own terminal. They never wait on the hook nor make it wait.

## Watch the agents live

Run `openmoat watch` in a second terminal pane:

```text
$ openmoat watch
OpenMoat 0.17.0 · watching Claude Code, Codex · policy 16 deny · 10 allow · 12 review

10:42:01  claude-code  ✓ Bash   cargo test -p openmoat-core            allow · dev-shell
10:42:07  claude-code  ✓ Edit   src/lib.rs                             allow · project-fs
10:42:15  claude-code  ? Bash   git config core.pager less             review · git-internals
                         ↳ changing git's config, hooks or refs · approve: openmoat allow --last
10:42:31  claude-code  ✕ Read   ~/.ssh/id_ed25519                      deny · secrets-paths
                         ↳ reading or changing your keys and credential files
10:42:40  codex        → Bash   make deploy                            defer · agent asks
```

- `✓` allowed, `?` held for review, `✕` denied, `→` left to the agent's own prompt. Times are UTC, as in `openmoat show`.
- A review or deny gets a second line: the rule's title, and for a review the command that approves it.
- Fewer calls: `--host <id>`, `--only deny` or `--only review`, `--session <id>`. `--recent N` sets how many earlier calls it shows first (10); `--no-follow` stops after them.
- Piped, each call is one plain line, for `grep`. Colour only on a terminal, never with `NO_COLOR`. Ctrl-C stops it.

## Why was this blocked?

`openmoat why` explains one decision: `--last` (the default) for the newest deny or review, an event id from `openmoat show` or `openmoat watch`, or a time today (UTC) as `watch` shows it, `10:42` or `10:42:31`.

```text
$ openmoat why --last
OpenMoat blocked: reading or changing your keys and credential files
  event   1f · 2026-11-06 10:42:31 UTC · claude-code · session 7c1e4b2a
  call    Read ~/.ssh/id_ed25519
  answer  moat: deny [secrets-paths] — secret material: read ~/.ssh/id_ed25519

Rules that decided it
  deny secrets-paths  reading or changing your keys and credential files
      why     a key or token that leaves this machine cannot be taken back
      reason  secret material: read ~/.ssh/id_ed25519
      from    your policy, ~/.moat/policy.yaml:17

Your policy now decides each part of the call as
  deny    read ~/.ssh/id_ed25519  (secrets-paths)

How to change it
  · `secrets-paths` is a deny rule of your policy (~/.moat/policy.yaml:17); only you can change it, with `openmoat edit`. An allow rule never overrides a deny

More: https://openmoat.dev/r/secrets-paths
```

| Section | What it says |
|---|---|
| Rules that decided it | each rule's title, `why`, the recorded reason, and where it is: your policy and line, your `openmoat allow` rules, the repository's `.moat/policy.yaml`, a policy pack, the policy's `defaults:` (no rule matched), or a built-in check |
| Your policy now decides | the call decided again, part by part, with the policy as it is now; if you changed the policy since, what would happen now |
| Context | what else shaped it: session taint, code the session wrote, text from another MCP server, a changed MCP server, OpenMoat's own files changed |
| How to change it | `openmoat edit` for a deny of your policy, `openmoat allow --last` (or `--always`) for the newest review, the repository's owners for its rules |

`--format json` prints the same as one document (`rules`, `decided_now`, `context`, `change`, `page`).

## Commands

| Command | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `openmoat watch [--host <id>] [--only deny\|review] [--session <id>] [--recent N] [--no-follow]` | Follow the agents' calls live, one line each | `openmoat watch --only deny` |
| `openmoat why [--last \| <id> \| HH:MM[:SS]] [--format json]` | Explain one deny or review | `openmoat why --last` |

## Limits

- Only what the hooks and the proxy recorded is shown: a call no hook reports is not in the log.
- `why` decides the call again with today's policy; a policy changed since may decide it differently, and it says so.
